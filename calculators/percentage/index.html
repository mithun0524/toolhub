Because each percentage applies to a different base. 100 rises by 50% to 150; a 50% fall from 150 is 75, not 100. To undo a rise of P% you need a fall of P / (100 + P) × 100 percent — 33.3% in this example. Mode 4 does that arithmetic for you.
Working backwards from the result to the original number. If a sale price of ₹1,700 is 85% of the original ticket price, mode 4 gives you the ₹2,000 you started from. This is the standard way to strip a discount or a tax out of a final figure.
Going from 5% to 6% is a rise of one percentage point but a 20% increase. Both are correct and they mean very different things — which is why financial reporting always names the unit explicitly.
