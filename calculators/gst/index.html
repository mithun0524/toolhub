Frequently asked questions

Adding GST treats your number as the pre-tax value and multiplies by the rate. Removing GST treats it as the final price already containing tax, so the taxable value is amount ÷ (1 + rate/100). This is the step people most often get wrong: 18% of an inclusive ₹11,800 is not ₹2,124, it is ₹1,800.
For a supply inside one state the total tax is split equally between central GST and state GST — 18% becomes 9% + 9%. For a supply across state lines, the whole amount is charged as a single integrated GST (IGST) of 18%. The total the customer pays is identical either way.
Slabs are set per HSN/SAC code: 5% for essentials and most food items, 12% and 18% for the bulk of goods and services, 28% for luxury and sin goods, plus special rates of 0.25% (rough diamonds) and 3% (gold and jewellery). Check your HSN code with a tax professional — rates are revised by the GST Council.
