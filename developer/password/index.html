It also uses rejection sampling rather than a modulo, because a plain modulo makes the first few characters of the alphabet slightly more likely and quietly reduces strength.
It measures how many guesses an attacker needs: each extra bit doubles the work. Under 40 bits is weak, 60–80 is solid for ordinary accounts, and 100+ is out of reach of any realistic offline attack. Length raises entropy faster than adding exotic symbols does.
It assumes an offline attacker with stolen password hashes trying 100 billion guesses per second — a well-funded GPU rig against a fast hash. Real attacks on a properly configured service are far slower, so treat the figure as a deliberately pessimistic floor.
